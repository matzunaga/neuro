About and credit

Henrietta Lacks
HeLa cells come from Henrietta Lacks, a Black American mother of five. In 1951, during treatment for cervical cancer at Johns Hopkins, cells from her tumor were taken for research without her knowledge or consent. She died that year, at 31. Her cells are still grown and studied around the world. Johns Hopkins on her story.
Cell
A cancer cell, not a healthy one. Wild-type HeLa (ATCC CCL-2) in interphase, the stage between divisions.

Whose cells
Jurkat cells come from the blood of a 14-year-old boy with T cell leukemia, in the mid-1970s. ATCC record.
Cell
An immortalized leukemia T cell, not a healthy immune cell. Jurkat clone E6-1 (ATCC TIB-152).
Scan
Focused ion beam scanning electron microscopy, 2018. The block is about 40 by 12 by 29 microns and holds several cells. This page shows one, cut out by hand, so its edges are approximate.

Changes
The published 3D surfaces were heavily simplified for phones, and flat faces at the edge of the block were removed. At most the largest 2,500 vesicle pieces, 1,200 endosome pieces and 700 microtubule pieces are shown.
Accuracy
The parts were labeled by software trained on hand-labeled samples, so some labels are wrong or incomplete.
Drawing
Color, lighting, shadow, blur and see-through surfaces are design choices. An electron microscope sees no color.
