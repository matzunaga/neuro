About and credit

What you see
One collision between protons at CERN’s Large Hadron Collider, as reconstructed by the CMS detector. The beams run along one axis and meet in the middle.
Why these
CERN picked these 13 events for education because the highlighted particles have a combined mass of 120 to 130 GeV, where the Higgs boson sits. They are candidates. Ordinary processes make events that look the same, so no single one is proof of a Higgs.
Colors
Red: muons in the highlighted group. Yellow: electrons or photons in the group. White: everything else. Dark dashes: missing momentum, what the detector could not account for sideways.
Layers
Tracks are charged particles. Hits are points where the tracker was touched. Energy is what calorimeter cells absorbed. Muon system shows chambers and segments on the outside. Jets are sprays of particles.
Data
Thomas McCauley for the CMS Collaboration, Higgs candidate events for use in education and outreach, CERN Open Data Portal, DOI 10.7483/OPENDATA.CMS.N9MJ.QEEC. Recorded in 2011 and 2012.

Limits
The files hold a subset of each event and are not suitable for physics analysis. The two-photon files carry no tracker hits or jets, so those events look sparser.
Drawing
Tracks are smooth curves between the stored end points and directions. Photons leave no track, so each is drawn as a straight line from its vertex to the calorimeter. Energy bar length and brightness grow with energy, capped. Jets appear above 20 GeV, as cones of a chosen shape. The faint rings are guides placed at the radius of the calorimeter cells. Colors, widths and the outward animation are design choices.
